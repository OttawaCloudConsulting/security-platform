## How the run was gated

1. A read-only copy of the recipe was cut at `# Step 3` (grep -c PATCH = 0). It re-listed the candidates, and they equal the approved 65.
2. The full recipe ran from a scratch copy that is byte-identical to the committed block except for the three placeholder lines. Its stdin came from a gate process. The gate waited for the recipe's own `Candidates (65):` listing, compared it with `candidate_ids`, and only then wrote `yes`. If the lists had differed it would have written `no`, so the recipe would have exited without changing anything. Gating on the recipe's own listing leaves no gap between the check and the write.
3. The recipe's `dd_api` exits on any non-200 response or TLS verification failure. The 65 `reset <id>` lines therefore mean 65 HTTP 200 PATCHes. The sorted ids equal the approved list.
